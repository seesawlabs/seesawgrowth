# Lead Generation Audit & Growth Plan (full)

*Prepared 2026-07-22*

## 0. Executive summary

**The math first.** $3.5M → $5M is a $1.5M gap ≈ 3 new engagements at $45k/mo running 12
months (or 4-5 running 6-9 months). This is not a volume game. You need roughly 4-6 genuinely
qualified opportunities per quarter, not 500 visitors a day. Every recommendation below is
filtered through that lens.

**The verdict on "do we show up":** No — but neither does anyone in your competitive set. All
six audited domains (you, PressW, Crux, Black Flag, Fuego, Caxy) get under ~100 organic US
visits/month. Your visibility problem is real, but it's an open flank, not a deficit you're
behind on. The bigger problems are upstream of traffic:

1. **Positioning:** "All-In-One Digital Product Invention Partner" says nothing to a buyer
   with a $500k AI budget. AI is one bullet among 14 in "Things We Do."
2. **Offer:** You sell undifferentiated services with no named entry product, no
   engagement-shape signals, no price qualification. Every strong competitor sells a named
   first step that structurally leads to the long engagement.
3. **Proof:** Your case studies are story-led, nearly metric-free. The one hard number you own
   — HPS's 5x reduction in medication approval times — is buried in a card subtitle instead of
   being a headline.

**The recommended position:** Design-led AI product studio for care-operations companies —
pharmacy/medication workflows, post-acute, dialysis, care management — "We take AI from
stalled pilot to production products people actually use." This exploits: (a) the market's
dominant buying narrative (MIT: 95% of internal GenAI pilots show no P&L impact;
externally-partnered builds succeed ~2x as often), (b) PressW's — your closest and best
competitor — total absence of a design/UX story, and (c) your existing case-study density
(HPS, Rendevor, Diet ID) in a sub-vertical no strong player currently owns. A follow-up
density check of the healthcare-AI agency quadrant confirmed the broad "healthcare AI
development company" label is contested — Light-it, NineTwoThree, plus ~20 offshore content
machines — while the mid-market care-operations quadrant is empty. (See the targeting report,
`03-targeting-report.md`.)

**The channel mix at your budget:** referral systematization + founder-led LinkedIn +
Anthropic partner track + a focused SEO/AEO play on keywords you can actually win + directory
hygiene (not sponsorship). Skip broad paid ads. Hire a fractional growth lead, not a CMO.

## 2. The market shift (and why your instinct is right)

- **Spend is bifurcating, not shrinking.** Enterprise GenAI services spend is growing ~73%
  CAGR (IDC: $16B 2023 → $143B 2027); McKinsey pegs AI tech-services at $200B+ by 2029 —
  concentrated in consulting, integration, and managed AI ops, not raw coding hours. Meanwhile
  the low end is being gutted by AI site builders and vibe-coding; generalist mid-tier shops
  are the ones squeezed.
- Only **13% of agencies have actually cut prices** (Productive.io survey of 180+ agencies);
  the ones seeing real AI leverage are raising them. The move is from hourly to
  fixed-fee/value pricing — AI compresses delivery 3-4x, so hourly billing punishes you for
  being good.
- **The dominant buying narrative is pilot failure.** MIT's GenAI Divide report: 95% of
  enterprise GenAI pilots show no measurable P&L impact; externally-partnered builds succeed
  ~67% vs ~33% for internal. Your buyer has already been burned once. This is the single
  strongest sales story available to you, and it's exactly what a design+engineering studio
  that ships production software should own.
- **Platform partner programs became real channels in 2026.** Anthropic launched a $100M
  partner network (Select tier: ~10 Claude-certified staff, 2 production deployments, 1 public
  case study — achievable at your size); OpenAI committed $150M; Vercel and AWS/GCP have
  certification tracks. Fractional AI — an AI implementation boutique — got effectively
  absorbed by Anthropic. PressW wears the "Official Anthropic Partner" badge on every service
  block in your city.
- **Buyers increasingly research agencies through LLMs.** Crux ships an "Ask AI About Us"
  ChatGPT deep-link; Fuego publishes crawlable FAQ blocks engineered for LLM citation. Your
  highest-intent page (AI Consulting) is invisible to both crawlers and LLMs.

**Implication:** the wave you sensed ("we don't talk about AI enough") is real, but the
winning move is not "add AI to the site." It's restructuring positioning, offer, and proof
around production AI outcomes — then making that visible in the places 2026 buyers actually
look (referral networks, LinkedIn, partner directories, LLM answers, and a handful of winnable
SERPs).

## 3. Ideal customer profile

**Primary ICP — "the burned buyer":**

- **Company:** $50M-$1B revenue (or PE-backed portfolio co), US, bias toward
  regulated/complex: healthcare, insurtech, fintech, logistics, professional services
- **Buyer:** CTO / VP Engineering / CEO (owner-led mid-market), increasingly Chief AI Officer;
  in PE contexts, the operating partner
- **Trigger:** internal AI pilot stalled before production; board pressure for visible AI ROI
  with capital discipline; a competitor shipped something
- **Budget reality:** mid-market AI initiatives typically run $100k-$500k. $40-50k/mo is the
  top of that band — it only makes sense framed as an **embedded product team** (product +
  design + engineering pod with AI leverage), never as consulting hours. Benchmark: typical
  mid-market AI retainers are $10-20k/mo; financial-services retainers hit $20-40k/mo. You're
  selling a team, not advice.

**Secondary ICP — the funded founder/scale-up** (your historical bread and butter: Kountable,
LineDance, Huddl): keep serving them, but they're the $10-20k/mo tier, not the growth engine.
Route them to smaller packages; don't let them consume BD capacity.

**Anti-ICP (say no):** sub-$10k discovery-only projects, pure staff-aug (your own strategy memo
already says this), idea-stage founders with no funding.

**Where these buyers look:** referrals first (always, at this ticket), then LinkedIn, then
Google/LLM research where directories and listicles function as validation, then platform
partner directories once they've picked a stack.

## 5. Offer architecture (the ladder)

This is the pattern every winning comp runs (PressW: Audit → Embedded Pods → Managed AI;
Morningside: $60k audit → $250k builds; Caxy: free Systems Check → $50k+ engagements). Your
version:

**Step 0 — Free: "AI Reality Check"** (30-45 min consult). Your idea, validated — as
top-of-funnel only. Diagnostic conversation + a one-page take. Booked via a real scheduling
link, qualified by a short intake form (revenue band, trigger, timeline).

**Step 1 — Paid, named entry product: "AI Production Roadmap" — $20-25k, 3 weeks.** Deep
discovery (your Megamine engine is literally a discovery machine — weaponize it internally),
opportunity map scored by ROI, architecture blueprint, adoption/UX assessment (your
differentiator — nobody else's audit includes "will humans actually use this"), and a build
plan. **100% credited against a build engagement signed within 90 days.** This is your other
idea, validated — paid-with-credit beats free: it filters tire-kickers, de-risks the buyer's
board conversation, and pre-sells the retainer. Caxy's free version works only because they
have a demand-gen machine to absorb the junk leads; you don't yet.

**Step 2 — The core: "Embedded AI Product Pod" — $40-50k/mo, 6-12 months.** Named team shape
(product lead + designer + 2 engineers + AI leverage), 90-days-to-production commitment, weekly
demo cadence, metric-defined outcomes. This is the thing the whole funnel exists to sell — give
it a page, a team diagram, and a duration expectation, like Fuego publishes ("typical
engagements run 6-12 months; retainer options available").

**Step 3 — Recurring tail: "Managed AI" — $8-15k/mo.** Model/agent ops, eval monitoring, cost
optimization, continuous improvement post-launch. Converts every pod into ongoing revenue and
smooths the portfolio-concentration problem.

**Publish qualification signals:** "Engagements typically start at $50k" (Caxy publishes
exactly this; Black Flag publishes full tiers). It will cost you some small deals. That's the
point — you said yourself you don't want $10k/2-week engagements, so stop marketing in a way
that invites them.

## 9. The CMO question

**Don't hire a CMO.** At $3.5M, a real CMO is $250-350k+ loaded — that's 25%+ of your entire
growth gap spent on strategy overhead when what you need is execution against a now-defined
strategy (this document is the strategy).

**Do hire: a fractional growth lead / senior marketing operator ($6-8k/mo** from your budget)
who owns: the content/LinkedIn engine, SEO contractor management, directory/review ops, the
named-account list, CRM hygiene, and reporting. Founder-led voices (you + Jeff) stay the face;
the operator makes the machine run weekly. Revisit a fractional CMO at ~$5M+ if channel
complexity demands it.

**On offering marketing services to clients: no, not now.** It dilutes the position you're
about to stake out ("55,000 agencies" problem), adds a delivery competency you'd be learning on
client dime, and your growth gap is closed by 3 pods, not a new service line. The adjacent move
that *does* fit: growth/adoption instrumentation as part of every pod ("we measure whether
people use what we ship") — that's design-led differentiation, not a marketing department.

## 10. What was missing from your framing

Your question list was traffic-first. At your deal size, the funnel is **trust-first.**
Specifically missing:

1. **Conversion infrastructure before traffic.** Speed-to-lead, a real scheduler, qualifying
   intake, CRM with stage definitions, nurture for the 6-18 month laggards. Traffic into a
   generic contact form is wasted spend.
2. **Proof before promotion.** Metric-led case studies are the asset every channel routes to.
   Two weeks of case-study work outperforms two months of ad spend right now.
3. **Offer design as marketing.** "What should we show up for" matters less than "what can a
   buyer say yes to." A named $20-25k audit with build credit is a marketing asset in itself.
4. **Platform partner programs as a channel.** Anthropic/OpenAI/Vercel directories didn't exist
   as real channels 18 months ago. They do now, they're achievable at your size, and your
   closest competitor is already monetizing one.
5. **AEO** — buyers now ask LLMs for agency shortlists. Your most important page is invisible
   to them.
6. **Pricing/engagement transparency as qualification.** You told me the deal shape you don't
   want; your site invites exactly it.
7. **Referral systematization.** Your best historical channel has no owner, no cadence, no
   language. Cheapest fix on this list.
8. **Sales capacity.** Who runs 4-6 qualified first calls a quarter, and the audits behind
   them? If it's you and Jeff, protect that time like delivery time.
9. **A measurement definition.** Track: qualified opportunities/quarter (target 4-6), audit
   conversions, audit→pod rate, pipeline $ by source, and referral-network touches — not traffic.
10. **The concentration guardrail.** Three $45k/mo clients out of ~$5M recreates the
    concentration problem you're escaping. The Managed AI tail (§5, Step 3) and keeping 2-3
    smaller engagements running is deliberate portfolio design, not distraction.
