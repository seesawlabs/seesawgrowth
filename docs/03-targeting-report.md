## 1. Wedge check: is "healthcare-AI product studio" already taken?

**Verdict: crowded in appearance, underpopulated in substance** — hold the wedge but narrow it
one notch, from "healthcare" to "care operations." The main Growth Plan has been amended
accordingly.

**The SEO noise floor:** ScienceSoft, LeewayHertz, Appinventiv, Andersen, Chetu, Arkenea,
Ideas2IT, Achievion, SoluLab, OpenXcell, Radixweb, and a dozen more. Every "top healthcare AI
development company" listicle is written by one of these firms about itself. Roughly 70% of the
visible category is offshore/nearshore content machines competing on volume and rates.

**The key profiles**

- **Light-it — the one that matters.** "Healthcare Strategy & Software Engineering — Built for
  Scale, Compliance, and Reliability." Healthcare-exclusive,
  discovery→design→build→data→EHR-migration ladder, explicit AI-governance stance, clients incl.
  Roche and Best Buy Health, budget floor $50k-150k. **Weakness vs. SeeSaw:** clientele is
  VC-backed digital-health startups and pharma innovation teams, not mid-market operational
  healthcare businesses; delivery is LatAm.
- **NineTwoThree** sells exactly SeeSaw's proposed motion (paid discovery sprint → validation →
  production build → scale) but its 2026 homepage leads FanDuel/Experian/SimpliSafe — it walked
  away from healthcare-first.
- **GoInvo** proves the design bar and that adoption metrics sell (Ipsos AI tool: 90% adoption,
  700k prompts/mo) but takes no build revenue.
- **HatchWorks/TATEEDA** are the cost-arbitrage tier: real healthcare AI engineering, no design
  leadership, no US-embedded partnership.

**What this means**

1. **SeeSaw cannot win the words "healthcare AI development company"** — that SERP is a
   listicle war. Rules out generic healthcare-AI SEO as a growth channel; proof-led outbound +
   referral networks within the sub-verticals instead.
2. **Nobody serves mid-market care operators** — hospice, specialty pharmacy, dialysis/renal,
   post-acute, care management. Startup-focused boutiques (Light-it), horizontal AI studios
   (NineTwoThree), design-only firms (GoInvo/Guidea), and enterprise firms (Wizeline, Veranex)
   all miss them. That's where SeeSaw's proof (HPS 5x, Rendevor, Diet ID) already sits.
3. **"Pilot-to-production" is fine narrative, weak differentiator** — EY, Workato, Wizeline all
   use the trope. Lead with named outcomes ("5x faster medication approvals"), not the phrase.
4. **Pricing validated:** Light-it's floor is $50k+; NineTwoThree sells paid validation sprints;
   the offshore tier can't deliver a design-led US-embedded pod at any rate. The $20-25k roadmap
   → $40-50k/mo pod ladder differentiates against the cheap tier rather than competing with it.
5. **Watch Light-it.** If they push upmarket from startups into operators, they take this lane
   with more content and lower rates. Speed matters more than message-polish.

## 4. The repeatable monthly targeting playbook

What actually worked (repeat monthly; ~2-3 hours or one standing agent task):

1. **Vendor-partnership press releases are the #1 trigger signal.** `[vertical] + "AI" +
   "partnership" press release 2026` surfaced the best Tier-A triggers (Compassus×Nestmed,
   Navitus×Banjo, Shields×Foundation Health, Senderra×Hyperscience, Evergreen×Phamily). A company
   that just bought a point-solution AI vendor has budget, a mandate, and adjacent unbuilt
   workflows — ideal timing. Standing watch: nestmed.com/news, Banjo Health, Foundation Health
   partner announcements.
2. **Vertical trade press beats general news:** Hospice News, Home Health Care News, HIT
   Consultant, Becker's, TechTarget HIMSS roundups — each yields 3-5 named accounts per query.
3. **PE portfolio pages + deal trackers** (thehealthcareinvestor.com, ctacquisitions.com roll-up
   trackers, havencrest.com, trivecapital.com): reliable Tier-B feed. Warmest sponsor doors:
   Havencrest (Dallas) and Trive (Dallas, explicit AI thesis via Veltris); Martis publicly
   investing in AI. Webster/Shore show no AI programs — their portcos stay Tier C.
4. **Texas roundups:** Dallas Innovates "AI 75" and the weekly Austin bio+health roundup (Jason
   Scharf, LinkedIn) — recurring dated feeds of funded local companies for hometown outreach.
5. **Least productive:** job-board searches (surface giants only) and TPA/benefits-admin queries
   (no verifiable AI announcements found — re-check manually). "Failed pilot" language is rarely
   public; proxy it by flagging companies that announced pilots 12+ months ago with no follow-up
   news.

**Caveats:** ownership/revenue marked (est.) not verified against filings; TPA/benefits and
stalled-pilot signals underrepresented; a few entries exceed the $1B band and are flagged. Verify
before outreach anything marked (verify).
