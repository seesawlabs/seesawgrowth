## What the audit actually found: three problems, all upstream of visibility

**01 · Nobody in our category shows up — including our competitors.** All six audited
domains (us, PressW, Crux, Black Flag, Fuego, Caxy) get under ~100 organic US visits a
month. We rank #3–4 for "outsourced CTO" off a single guide, and we rank for *zero* AI
keywords. This is an open flank, not a deficit we're behind on.

**02 · Our positioning says nothing to a buyer with a $500k AI budget.** "All-In-One Digital
Product Invention Partner," with AI as one bullet among fourteen in "Things We Do." Our
highest-intent page — AI Consulting — is almost entirely the AI Jeff gimmick with nearly zero
crawlable substance. It can't rank, and it can't be cited when a CTO asks Claude "who should
build our AI product?"

**03 · Our proof is story-led and nearly metric-free.** The one hard number we own — HPS's
5x reduction in medication approval times — is buried in a card subtitle instead of being the
headline. Kountable's "$8MM Series B" is in a subtitle too. The raw material exists; we've
never made it the point.

> **The market fact that makes this urgent:** MIT's GenAI Divide report found 95% of
> enterprise GenAI pilots show no measurable P&L impact — and that externally-partnered
> builds succeed roughly 67% of the time versus 33% for internal ones.
>
> Our buyer has already been burned once. That is the single strongest sales story available
> to us, and it is exactly what a design + engineering studio that ships production software
> should own.

## Decision 1 — Positioning
*Decision needed today · everything else blocks on it*

**Recommendation:** Design-led AI product studio, with a care-operations wedge — pharmacy and
medication workflows, post-acute and hospice, dialysis and renal, care management.

> **"We take AI from stalled pilot to production."**
> SeeSaw Labs designs and ships AI products people actually use — for pharmacy, post-acute,
> and care-operations companies where "it demos well" isn't good enough.

**Why this one**

- **PressW is our closest and best competitor, and their gap is design.** Anthropic Partner
  badge, 70+ shipped projects, an Audit → Pods → Managed AI ladder — and no UX story anywhere
  on their site. We will not out-PressW PressW on generic applied AI. We can own the quadrant
  they're missing.
- **Adoption is where enterprise AI actually dies.** "No P&L impact" means people didn't use
  the thing. "AI products people actually adopt" is a genuinely underpopulated position, and
  it's our 15-year DNA.
- **Our proof already lives in healthcare.** HPS, Rendevor, Diet ID, Huddl, Archway, Hologic,
  Legacy Community Health — plus real SEO authority in the CCDA/interoperability cluster. We
  don't have to *become* a healthcare specialist. We have to admit we are one.
- **The wedge narrowed one notch after a density check.** Broad "healthcare AI" is contested
  — Light-it, NineTwoThree, and roughly 70% offshore content machines writing their own "top
  healthcare AI companies" listicles. But mid-market care *operators* are served by nobody
  with a design-led, US-embedded build model.

**What it costs us.** We stop leading with "we do everything." This is healthcare-first in
*proof and content*, not healthcare-only in sales — we still take the Bacardis and the
Kountables. And we keep Fractional CTO as a named offering, rebranded for the era, because
it's our only working SEO funnel and a natural Trojan horse into pods.

**The risk worth naming.** Light-it (Montevideo/NYC, healthcare-exclusive, $50k+ floor)
currently sells VC-backed digital-health startups and pharma innovation teams. If they push
upmarket into operators, they take this lane with more content and lower rates. Speed to
plant the flag beats perfecting the messaging.

## Decision 2 — The website
*Decision: do we rebuild in this order*

**Recommendation:** Proof before promotion. Two weeks of case-study work outperforms two
months of ad spend at our stage — so the case studies come before any traffic work, not after it.

1. **Six case studies rebuilt, metric-as-headline.** HPS ("5x faster medication approvals"),
   Kountable ("whiteboard to $8MM Series B"), Rendevor, LineDance/Movality, Diet ID, and one
   internal AI-ops story. "Est." hedging wherever it's honest. Film 2–3 as 90-second
   client-voice videos.
2. **Homepage:** new hero on the pilot-to-production line, a proof bar, the offer ladder,
   metric-led case cards. Kill the 14-item "Things We Do" list — group into 3–4
   outcome-framed capabilities with AI first.
3. **AI Production Roadmap offer page** + a real booking flow: scheduler plus qualifying
   intake, not a generic contact form.
4. **Rebuild /ai-consulting** with actual crawlable substance — methodology, case studies,
   credentials. Keep AI Jeff as garnish, not the meal.
5. **Fractional CTO service page** to capture the rankings we already have, bridged to AI:
   "Fractional CTO for the AI era."
6. **Embedded Pod page** with team shape, duration, and "engagements start at $50k."
7. **FAQ/AEO blocks sitewide** + an "Ask AI about us" deep-link. When a CTO asks Claude for
   AI firms with healthcare experience, we want to be citable.

Right now a $5k prospect and a $500k prospect fill out the same form. There's no lead magnet,
no booking link, no named offer, no qualification. Conversion infrastructure has to exist
before any traffic work or the traffic is wasted.

## Decision 3 — The offer
*Decision: approve Steps 0 and 1, and the published price floor*

**Recommendation:** Build the full ladder, but today's decision is only the free consult and
the paid entry product. Every winning competitor sells a named first step that structurally
leads to the long engagement.

| Step | Offer | Price | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 · Free** *(decide today)* | AI Reality Check | 30–45 min | A diagnostic conversation plus a one-page written take. Booked through a real scheduling link, qualified by a short intake — revenue band, trigger, timeline. Top-of-funnel only. |
| **1 · Paid** *(decide today)* | AI Production Roadmap | $20–25k / 3 wks | Deep discovery, an ROI-scored opportunity map, architecture blueprint, adoption/UX assessment, and a build plan. 100% credited against a build engagement signed within 90 days. |
| **2 · Core** | Embedded AI Product Pod | $40–50k/mo | 6–12 months. Product lead + designer + 2 engineers + AI leverage. 90-days-to-production commitment, weekly demo cadence, metric-defined outcomes. This is the thing the whole funnel exists to sell. |
| **3 · Tail** | Managed AI | $8–15k/mo | Model and agent ops, eval monitoring, cost optimization, continuous improvement post-launch. Converts every pod into recurring revenue. |

**Two things that make it work**

- **Paid-with-credit beats free at Step 1.** It filters tire-kickers, de-risks the buyer's
  board conversation, and pre-sells the retainer. Caxy's free version works because they have
  a demand-gen machine to absorb the junk leads. We don't have one yet.
- **The adoption/UX assessment is the differentiator.** Nobody else's audit asks "will humans
  actually use this." That's the whole thesis in one deliverable line item.

**The uncomfortable part.** Publish "engagements typically start at $50k." It will cost us
some small deals — that's the point. We've said we don't want $10k, two-week engagements, so
we should stop marketing in a way that invites them.

## Known tensions (being honest about it)

- **Three $45k/mo clients out of ~$5M recreates the concentration problem we're trying to
  escape.** The Managed AI tail and deliberately keeping 2–3 smaller engagements running is
  portfolio design, not distraction. Worth agreeing on the guardrail now rather than at $5M.
- **Narrowing costs us inbound breadth in the short term.** The bet is that 4–6 qualified
  opportunities beat 40 unqualified ones at this ticket size. If that bet is wrong, we'll
  know by day 90.
- **I'm recommending we don't build a marketing service line for clients.** It dilutes the
  position we're about to stake out, adds a competency we'd be learning on client dime, and
  our gap closes with 3 pods — not a new offering. The adjacent move that *does* fit is
  adoption instrumentation inside every pod.
- **The underlying data is from the July 22 audit.** The competitive and positioning findings
  hold. The keyword volumes and difficulty scores should be re-pulled before we spend
  anything on SEO.
